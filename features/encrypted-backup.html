Encrypted backups to your own Google Drive or iCloud

A backup protects your documents if the phone is lost, broken or replaced. Docsly encrypts it on the phone, under a passphrase you choose, before it goes anywhere — and it goes to your cloud account, not to ours. We do not have one.

Where the backup goes

  • Android: your Google Drive. The backup is a file in your Drive, which you can see and delete like any other file.
  • iPhone and iPad: your iCloud.
  • Any platform: Export to file saves the same kind of archive, encrypted under a passphrase, anywhere you like — a computer, a USB drive, another cloud.

How to make one

  1. Open Settings → Data & storage → Backup & Restore. On Android, tap Sign in with Google first; on iPhone, be signed in to iCloud.
  2. Tap Create Backup and enter a passphrase of at least 8 characters. Keep it somewhere safe.
  3. Choose Full backup, or Choose what to include to back up only some folders.

Backups are made when you start them; Docsly does not back up on a schedule.

The passphrase

The archive is encrypted with AES-256-GCM under a key derived from your passphrase with PBKDF2 (600,000 iterations). The passphrase never leaves the phone and is never stored with the backup. Nobody can recover it — not even us. Without it, the backup cannot be opened; that is the point of it.

Restoring

On a new phone, install Docsly, open Backup & Restore and restore from your Drive or iCloud, or use Import from file. You can restore everything or pick what to bring back, and choose whether it replaces what is on the phone or is added to it. Recognised text is not stored in the backup; the documents are read again after the restore. Step by step: Restore a backup.

Between Android and iPhone

Google Drive is not visible from an iPhone and iCloud is not visible from Android. To move to the other platform, export to a file and open it on the new phone, or send the documents over Wi-Fi.

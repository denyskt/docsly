Text recognition and search inside documents

Docsly reads the text in your photos, scans and PDFs on the phone itself, so you can find a contract by its number or an insurance policy by the insurer’s name — even with no connection at all.

Search by any word

Search on the Files and Favorites tabs and in any folder looks at document names and at the text inside the documents. A document found only by its text is marked Found in the text. Search ignores letter case and accents, and matches a Latin spelling against Cyrillic, so “Ivanov” also finds “Иванов”.

Fields you can copy

For passports, ID cards, driving licences and bank documents Docsly picks out the fields people copy most often: document number, names, dates of birth, issue and expiry, nationality, personal number, licence categories, address, issuing authority, IBAN and card number. Fields read from the machine-readable zone of a passport or ID card are marked Verified by check digit. The same expiry date drives the expiry reminders.

On the phone, without internet

Nothing is sent anywhere to be read. On iPhone and iPad, recognition uses Apple’s Vision framework; on Android, Google’s ML Kit models built into the app, plus Tesseract for Cyrillic and Thai. The recognised text is stored encrypted, like the documents.

Languages on Android: Latin-script languages, Cyrillic (Russian, Ukrainian), Chinese, Japanese, Korean, Thai, and Hindi and other Devanagari text. On iPhone and iPad the set depends on the iOS version: the main Latin-script languages, Cyrillic, Chinese, Japanese and Korean are read, while some languages — Thai, Turkish or Polish, for example — may need a newer iOS. Hindi is not read on iPhone and iPad.

Turning it on

Settings → Features → Text recognition. It is on for new installs; if you updated from an older version, switch it on there. Recognition runs while the app is open and unlocked.

What it does not do

  • Only the first 20 pages of a document are read.
  • Recognition does not run in the background, only while Docsly is open.
  • The recognised text is not part of backups or .docsly files. After a restore, the documents are read again on the new phone.

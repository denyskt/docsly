Send documents between Android and iPhone over Wi-Fi

Docsly moves documents from one phone to another over the Wi-Fi network both phones are on. It works the same from Android to iPhone, from iPhone to Android, and between two phones of the same kind. Nothing goes through a server or a cloud.

How it works

  1. On the phone that receives, open Receive over Wi-Fi from the “…” menu on the Folders or Files tab, and keep that screen open.
  2. On the phone that sends, choose a document, a folder or several documents and tap Send over Wi-Fi.
  3. Pick the other phone in Nearby devices. If it does not appear, the receiving phone can show a QR code for the sending phone to scan.
  4. Both screens show the same six-digit code. Check that it matches, and on the sending phone tap Code matches.
  5. The receiving phone sees who is sending, how many documents and how large, and taps Accept or Decline.

What is sent

The documents you chose, folders with their subfolders, and the tags those documents carry. Hidden and locked subfolders go too, unless you untick Include hidden folders or Include locked folders. Documents sent on their own land in the receiving phone’s Inbox. The received documents are added to what is already there; nothing on the receiving phone is replaced.

Recognised text does not travel: the receiving phone reads the documents again on its own.

How it is protected

The two phones agree on keys made for this one transfer (ECDH P-256), and the data is encrypted with AES-256-GCM on the way. The six-digit code is what proves that you are talking to your own phone and not to someone else on the network, so compare it before you tap.

The phones talk to each other directly inside your network. No internet connection is needed, and no copy is left anywhere else.

What it does not do

  • Both phones must be on the same Wi-Fi network. Guest and office networks that keep devices apart, and a VPN that routes all traffic away, stop the phones from seeing each other.
  • One transfer is up to 4 GB or 100,000 documents. For more, send a .docsly file instead.
  • It is not syncing and not a backup: it copies once, when you send.
  • While the receive screen is open, other devices on the same network can see the name you chose for Wi-Fi transfer.

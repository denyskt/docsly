Passports, contracts, insurance — organized and encrypted on your phone

The documents you actually need, warranties and receipts included — in one app, sorted the way you think about them, and encrypted on your device. Docsly has no account and no server.

  • AES-256 on the device

New in Docsly 1.4

Docsly reads your documents

Text recognition runs on your device, offline. The recognized text never leaves it and is stored encrypted, like the documents themselves.

Find a document by any word inside it

A number, a surname, a line from the page — search finds the passport, contract or receipt that contains it, not only the one with that name.

Copy the number you need in one tap

Docsly picks out the fields of a passport or ID — number, names, dates — so you copy them instead of retyping. A field is marked as checked when the check digits of the machine-readable zone agree.

Driving licences from the US, Canada and Europe are read too.

Know before a passport expires

In the last month before a passport, ID card or driving licence expires, its card shows the days left. Docsly reminds you 30, 10 and 3 days before, on the day and the day after.

Text recognition, fields and expiry reminders are in Docsly 1.4 for iPhone and iPad. On Android they come with the next update.

Privacy

A vault, not another cloud folder

Document contents are encrypted on your device with AES-256. The key is protected by the iOS Keychain or the Android KeyStore and never leaves the device.

  • PIN, Face ID, Touch ID or fingerprint
  • A second PIN opens a separate, hidden vault
  • Encrypted backups under a passphrase you choose — in your own cloud account or in a file, never through us

How the encryption works · Privacy policy

No accountNothing to sign up for, no email to give.
No serverDocsly has no backend. Your documents are never uploaded; the app sends only anonymous usage and crash data (privacy policy).
Works offlineYour documents, search and text recognition work with no connection.
One thing to know up front: a backup passphrase cannot be recovered — not by us, not by anyone. That is what makes a backup safe to keep in your own cloud, and why you should write the passphrase down somewhere you trust.
